# Youth Lawn & Yard Crew Business Plan Outline

## 1. Core business concept

**Business type:**
Local student-run lawn maintenance and light yard cleanup service.

**Positioning:**
Reliable, simple, neighborhood lawn and yard help by local students.

**What they are not:**
They are not a full landscaping company, tree service, pesticide applicator, hauling company, or pressure-washing contractor.

**Primary goal:**
Build a manageable summer business that teaches customer service, responsibility, job costing, route planning, teamwork, and money management without putting them into high-liability work.

# 4. Services excluded

These should be explicitly excluded from the business.

| Excluded work                    | Reason                                   |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Tree trimming                    | High risk, skill issue, property damage. |
| Chainsaw work                    | Not appropriate for this youth business. |
| Roof/gutter work                 | Fall risk.                               |
| Ladder work                      | Unnecessary liability.                   |
| Pesticides/herbicides/fertilizer | Licensing and safety issues.             |
| Retaining walls/hardscaping      | Outside skill/scope.                     |
| Major hauling/dump runs          | Vehicle, disposal, and liability issues. |
| Heavy brush clearing             | Tool and injury risk.                    |
| House siding pressure washing    | Damage risk.                             |
| Deck pressure washing            | Damage risk.                             |
| Unknown trash/hazard cleanup     | Safety risk.                             |

Ohio law specifically exempts minors engaged in “lawn mowing, snow shoveling, and other related employment” from the minor labor chapter, but that should not be treated as permission to let them drift into dangerous work. Keep the scope boring and safe. ([Ohio Laws][1])

# 8. Payment model

## Accepted payment methods

* Cash
* Venmo

## Important Venmo note

For minors, this needs parent involvement. Venmo says personal accounts require the user to be at least 18 or age of majority, while Venmo Teen Accounts are for ages 13–17 and must be parent/legal-guardian linked. ([Venmo Help Center][2]) Venmo business profile payments have seller transaction fees, commonly listed as **1.9% + $0.10** for direct payments from another Venmo account. ([Venmo Help Center][3])

## Recommended setup

Best practical approach:

* Parent/guardian oversees the payment account.
* App records the payment method.
* App does **not** process payments directly.
* App shows payment instructions:

  * Cash due on completion
  * Venmo handle or QR code
* Admin manually marks invoice as paid.

This avoids building payment processing into a simple youth business app.

## Payment statuses

| Status        | Meaning                                          |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Unpaid        | Job not paid yet.                                |
| Cash pending  | Customer said cash will be paid on-site.         |
| Venmo pending | Customer says Venmo was sent, not confirmed yet. |
| Paid          | Parent/admin confirms payment received.          |
| Partial       | Deposit or partial payment received.             |
| Problem       | Payment issue needs follow-up.                   |

# 9. Terms and conditions outline

This should be simple and parent-reviewed.

## Customer must agree that:

* Price must be approved before work starts.
* Payment is due when work is completed.
* Customer is responsible for clearing toys, hoses, tools, wires, and fragile items from the yard.
* Crew is not responsible for hidden objects left in grass.
* Crew may decline unsafe or out-of-scope work.
* No chemical application is offered.
* No ladders, roofs, tree cutting, or hazardous cleanup.
* Pets must be secured before arrival.
* Dog waste must be removed or separately quoted.
* Weather may delay service.
* Before/after photos may be taken for job documentation.
* Exact address and private customer information will not be publicly shared.
* Customer must disclose known hazards.

## Terms flow in app

Customer should accept terms at two points:

1. Before submitting a quote request.
2. Before accepting the final job estimate.

That second acceptance matters more because it attaches to a real price and scope.

## K. Earnings split calculator

The app should calculate:

1. Gross revenue
2. Job-specific expenses
3. Equipment reserve
4. Tax/savings reserve
5. Remaining profit
6. Split per person

Default split model:

| Category            |    Suggested amount |
| ------------------- | ------------------: |
| Equipment reserve   |                 10% |
| Tax/savings reserve |                 15% |
| Remaining split     | Equal among workers |

## Split adjustment

If only two boys worked a job, only those two split the labor profit.

If one family’s equipment was used heavily, they can record an equipment reimbursement before profit split.

# 17. Operating rules

## Quoting rules

* No job starts without a confirmed price.
* No open-ended cleanup jobs.
* No materials purchased without payment/deposit.
* Customer must approve any added work.
* Photos are required for questionable jobs.

## Work rules

* No one works alone at a stranger’s house.
* No jobs after dark.
* No entering homes.
* No ladders.
* No chemicals.
* No chainsaws.
* No aggressive dogs.
* No hazardous trash.
* No “quick extra” without requote.

## Payment rules

* Payment due same day.
* Cash counted and logged immediately.
* Venmo verified by parent/admin.
* Unpaid jobs do not get future service.

# Bottom line

The business should stay narrow:

> **Recurring mowing + light yard cleanup, managed through a simple quote/job/payment app with parent oversight.**

The app should not be fancy. It should answer five questions fast:

1. Who is the customer?
2. Where is the job?
3. What exactly are they paying for?
4. Has the customer accepted the terms and price?
5. Has the job been completed and paid?

That is enough. Everything beyond that is dessert, and most dessert makes teenagers slower.
